The constraint that shaped the palette

theme.css already states it: a hue that carries meaning "must not move with a taste preference." Green means healthy, amber means warning, red means danger, blue is both info and a config source. Five of those hues are also selectable accents. So identity colours cannot simply be picked from the wheel: a peer tinted red would read as a peer in trouble.

The way out is to separate identity from status by saturation, not hue. Status colours in this app are vivid. Identity tints are muted, sitting one step above the grey ramp rather than competing with the accent. That frees the whole wheel, including green and amber, because a muted sage at 10px does not read as "healthy".

How a name gets its colour

Not random per session. A hash of the name picks the index, so a peer keeps its colour for as long as it exists, across restarts and across both surfaces. Random-per-render would mean the same peer changed colour between the sidebar and the transcript, which is worse than no colour at all.

questionanswer
Two peers land on the same tint?Possible with six colours. Names sit next to a distinct title and timestamp, so a repeat is a mild collision, not an ambiguity.
Does it fight the accent?The accent owns fills, borders and the active row. These are text only, and muted, so they sit below it in the hierarchy.
What about a name with no peer?Unchanged. A row with no name still shows the time alone, exactly as today.

What I would not do

Not a coloured dot or a filled chip beside the name. That adds an element to a 10px subtitle already carrying three, and a filled shape at that size reads as a status indicator, which is the exact confusion the saturation split exists to avoid. Colouring the existing text costs no space.
